01 · The Problem

Tool wear is invisible until it isn't.

01
Current Situation

Tools are changed on a fixed schedule — every N parts, every shift — whether they need it or not.

→
02
Problem

Flank wear develops at the tool tip, inside the cut. No operator can see it forming.

→
03
Consequence

Too early → usable carbide life discarded. Too late → scrapped parts, drift, tool fracture risk.

→
04
Need

A continuous, physics-grounded wear signal an operator can trust — not a calendar guess.

Existing challenge Fixed schedules can't see actual tool condition — they're wrong in one direction every time.
Existing challenge Vendor monitoring hardware is expensive and hands over an opaque score nobody can verify.
02 · The Solution

From spindle telemetry to a tool-change decision.

Metrik reads live spindle telemetry, scores wear and risk with two independently validated models grounded in machining physics, explains every number, and hands the decision to the operator.

INPUT

Spindle Telemetry

  • Speed, torque, temperature, runtime
  • 3-axis force / vibration / acoustic emission
→
PROCESSING

Feature Engineering

  • Baseline-relative windowing
  • Missing-sensor fallback
→
INTELLIGENCE

Dual Model + Physics

  • XGBoost wear + risk models
  • Taylor life baseline + SHAP
→
OUTPUT

Decision-Ready Estimate

  • Wear % / risk % + confidence
  • Ranked drivers, remaining life
→
USER ACTION

Operator Decides

  • Acknowledge or log a change
  • Verdict feeds accuracy scoreboard
Every endpoint is read-and-recommend — Metrik never stops, throttles, or reconfigures a machine.

05 · The Innovation

Not "we use AI" — a specific, defensible combination.

Traditional

A single ML score, trained and tested on the same tool, presented with no explanation.

→
Gap

Random-split validation inflates results 10.7× — and operators can't verify or trust an opaque number.

→
Metrik's Innovation

Physics-grounded model + honest leave-one-tool-out validation + SHAP explanation + closed operator feedback loop.

→
Advantage

A number the operator can verify against physics, understand, and trust enough to act on.

01

Physics-Grounded

Checked against Taylor's tool-life equation (1907), not a pure black box.

02

Honest Validation

Reports its own 10.7× optimism gap instead of the flattering number.

03

Explainable

SHAP ranks the exact driver behind every prediction.

04

Closed Feedback Loop

Operator verdicts score the model with a Wilson-interval scoreboard.

06 · Impact

Who it helps, and what it changes.

Demonstrated

+19.3% Tool Life Recovered

Vs. a practical fixed schedule, in offline validation across three cutters — zero tools changed late.

Expected

Operators Act on Evidence

A live, explained wear reading at the machine replaces a calendar guess.

Expected

Waste Cut in Both Directions

Fewer scrapped parts from undetected wear, fewer tools discarded with life left in them.

Potential

Accessible to Small Shops

Standard telemetry, no proprietary sensor hardware — scales down-market where vendor suites can't.

Potential

Fleet-Wide Visibility

Plant managers plan maintenance windows around production, not around failures.
